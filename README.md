# AMR-among-hospitalized-patients-in-Nigeria-a-systematic-review-and-meta-analysis
# National burden and health system variations in antimicrobial resistance among hospitalized patients in Nigeria across successive National Action Plan Policy Phases: A systematic review and meta-analysis

## About this study

This repository contains the deidentified extracted dataset, R analytical scripts, and supporting materials accompanying a systematic review and meta-analysis investigating the burden and patterns of antimicrobial resistance (AMR) among hospitalized patients in Nigeria.

The study synthesises evidence from 185 studies involving 69,213 participants and 39,954 bacterial isolates. It examines AMR patterns across three National Action Plan (NAP) implementation periods: Pre-NAP, NAP 1.0, and early NAP 2.0.

In addition to estimating the pooled burden of antimicrobial resistance, the study investigates variations across geographical regions, healthcare facility types, hospital settings, bacterial pathogens, specimen types and antimicrobial classes. It also explores differences in resistance patterns across successive national policy implementation periods.

## Purpose of this repository

This repository is intended to promote transparency, facilitate reproducibility, and support further research on antimicrobial resistance in Nigeria and other comparable healthcare settings.

It provides access to the analytical materials underpinning the study, including:

- Deidentified study-level extracted data.
- R scripts for data cleaning, harmonisation and preparation.
- Effect-size calculations and statistical modelling procedures.
- Overall and subgroup meta-analyses.
- Sensitivity analyses and supporting analytical outputs.
- Supplementary materials and relevant documentation.

## Analytical approach

The analyses were conducted using R statistical software (version 4.6.1). Pooled estimates were generated using random-effects meta-analytic approaches, including multilevel models where appropriate, to account for the hierarchical structure of the extracted effect sizes. Between-study heterogeneity was assessed, and subgroup analyses were undertaken to explore variations in AMR estimates across relevant epidemiological and healthcare-system characteristics.

## Reproducibility and data sharing

The materials provided in this repository are intended to enable researchers to examine the analytical procedures, reproduce the reported findings, and build upon the evidence generated by this systematic review and meta-analysis.

The repository will be updated with relevant documentation and supporting materials where necessary.
